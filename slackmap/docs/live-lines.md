# Live lines

People did not put rigged lines on SlackMap because most lines are rigged unofficially, and nobody wants to be officially associated with one. A rig is a line reported as rigged now, until a date, then taken down. It only works if reporting cannot be tied to the reporter.

## The promise

ISA must be technically unable to know who reported a rig. The reason is liability: "we cannot know", not "we promise not to look". Legally only proof counts: a correlation, like who was signed in around the time of a report, is a smart guess that does not hold up officially, and the report stays anonymous. So what must never exist is a record tying a report to a person; making guesses harder is not worth much.

- A real member stands behind each report. The blind-signed pass proves that without saying which member; it is an industry standard kept simple, and reassuring to users who read about it.
- The rig routes never read the login, and their log lines carry nothing that could match them to the same browser's signed-in calls. Everything else keeps its logs as they are: analytics and debugging are not given up for this.
- Ownership is a manage link, not an account. Sharing it is how a rigger moves to another device or hands the rig to their crew, one mechanism for both.
- Reminders before expiry are a calendar event on the reporter's device and a banner on the card. ISA sends nothing.

## What a rig is

Rigging is an extension of a line that already exists. Creating a line stays the normal flow and says a line can be rigged there, not that anyone rigged it legally; only lines on the map can be reported rigged.

A rig is: rigged, until when, reported when. SlackMap serves slackliners; what consumers such as aviation do with the data is not its concern, so nothing is added for their sake.

Edge cases like a fake rig blocking a line until it expires are accepted, not designed around.
