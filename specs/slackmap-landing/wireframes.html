SlackMap landing page: wireframes

Most visitors never leave the map, so the map itself has to show what SlackMap offers: lines, spots, access guides, countries, recent activity, communities, adding data, and soon lines rigged right now. Three directions, each desktop and phone. Pick a direction, then we refine.

Today

Nine separate controls float at the edges, each with its own style; the desktop sidebar spends 240 px on two links. Activity, countries and communities are there but easy to miss.

A. Top bar + filter chips

Everything you control lives in one strip at the top: search, what to show, and add. The legend becomes those chips, so tapping "Spots" both explains the green color and filters to spots. The permanent sidebar goes; the map gets the full width.

B. Explore panel

The way Google Maps or Komoot work: the sidebar stops being a menu and becomes a living panel about what's on the map. It answers "what is here?" before anyone clicks: counts, what's rigged now, latest activity, and the communities nearby. Clicking a feature replaces the panel with its details; back returns to Explore.

C. Icon rail + bottom tabs

The app-like option: a slim rail on desktop and a tab bar on the phone list every part of SlackMap as a place to go. Clearest for someone hunting a feature; less "browse-first" than A or B.

Shared across all three, whichever you pick

  • The legend becomes the filter. One set of colored chips explains colors and filters at once; the layers dialog keeps only map style.
  • One search for places and SlackMap content (lines, spots, guides, countries, groups by name). Searching content is new: lines carry names, but the map file doesn't today.
  • One Add for line, spot, access guide and group; sign-in asked only on tap, as today.
  • Communities joins the main map as a switch or tab instead of a separate page in the sidebar.
  • "Rigged now" has a home from day one (the live lines spec).
  • Footer links (FAQ, contact, open source, terms) move into a help or account menu.
